Faster auto-stop and rejection of Blue's ground auto-start

Updated with pilot-confirmed October 27 landing times: Raccoon 13:19:15; Redstrap, Blue and v3.2.8 13:29:37 PDT. The previously reported launch-sequence trigger times were accepted by the pilot. No firmware changes have been made.

Auto-stop

A reasonable next candidate is 15 qualifying checks (approximately 15 seconds), speed below 3 mph, altitude strictly within 2 m of the retained anchor, and the existing ±0.2 g acceleration requirement. The existing no-GPS speed bypass is preserved. The altitude threshold is departure from a retained anchor, not a 2 m full rolling range; opposite excursions can span nearly 4 m.

Both this candidate and a more aggressive 10-second version produce no possible in-flight stops under the sampled-input reconstruction across all ten flight-containing recordings from October 25 and 27. The earlier recordings contain repeated views of the same flights; these are not ten independent flight conditions. With speed kept below 3 mph, the longest airborne speed/acceleration-only quiet runs are eight checks in the October 27 set and at most eight in the earlier set. Thus 10 checks has relatively little margin against unrecorded smooth, slow flight; 15 has more margin.

The current configuration's 21/23-second landing delays remain the earliest compatible delays, not observed auto-stops. Auto-stop was disabled. Widening the altitude tolerance to 2 m materially reduces sensitivity to small pressure fluctuations. Increasing the speed threshold to 4 mph does not improve these October 27 landing results, but permits longer airborne quiet runs. Retain 3 mph initially. A 15-second counter with the present halfway-warning rule gives roughly eight seconds to cancel; a 10-second counter gives about five. If a full ten-second warning is required, warning timing needs separate design.

Auto-start: how much tightening rejects Blue?

Blue's actual forward trigger occurred around 13:04:50. The three-second mean at that instant was 6.45 mph, but the following rolling mean rose to 7.22 mph. Therefore raising the threshold just above 6.45 mph does not reject the event. Later qualifying displacement also exceeds the initial 9.41 m and reaches 10.90 m.

With the existing one-second confirmation, rejection begins around a 6.89–6.9 mph speed threshold or a 10.09–10.1 m displacement threshold in this replay. Use practical margins such as 7 mph or 11 m rather than tuning to those narrow boundaries. A 6.5 mph speed threshold and a 10 m displacement threshold each still trigger. Confirmation alone must exceed the roughly 4.26-second qualifying run; five additional seconds rejects it, whereas four still triggers. A balanced alternative is 6 mph plus three additional confirmation seconds.

Keep the blended path's existing normalization separate if changing only the standalone forward test. The current firmware shares the 5 mph / 5 m scale with the blended path; simply changing that constant also makes the blended path less sensitive. That costs v3.2.8 about 11 seconds in these flights.

These results do not establish universal ground/flight separation. A level headwind launch at 5–6 mph would no longer qualify through a 7 mph standalone forward path; other paths help only when their vertical evidence is sufficient. Increasing displacement to 11 m within three seconds requires about 8.2 mph of straight-line motion, so it is a stronger low-speed restriction. A 6 mph / three-second confirmation alternative preserves sensitivity at 6 mph but takes about six seconds of ideal sustained motion in the forward-only case.

Method and saved results

Start sweeps use time-weighted windows from the actual C++ implementation. Precise pre-start diagnostics allow exact replay of Blue's original trigger; counterfactual later windows use rounded B pressure altitude and VAR. Post-start CV is an available proxy for the one-second-average validity flag. Stop sweeps retain the current counter/anchor algorithm, current acceleration gate, and no-GPS fallback; all possible integer-centimeter pressures consistent with each positive whole-meter B altitude are propagated through retained-anchor/count states. Stop states are absorbed to bound the first stop. Results assume the sampled B/GL/GA inputs represent each stop check and no warning is canceled. These bounds are model estimates, not device guarantees or actual stop timestamps.
